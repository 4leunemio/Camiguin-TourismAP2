About Camiguin

Learn about the Island Born of Fire — its geography, people, government, and what makes it one of the Philippines' most extraordinary destinations.

The Island Born of Fire

Camiguin is a small volcanic island province located in the Bohol Sea, off the northern coast of Mindanao in the Philippines. It is the second-smallest province in the country by land area, covering approximately 238 square kilometers, yet it boasts more volcanoes per square kilometer than any other island on Earth. The island has five volcanoes, the most prominent and active being Mt. Hibok-Hibok.

The province is divided into five municipalities: Mambajao (the provincial capital), Catarman, Guinsiliban, Mahinog, and Sagay. With a population of over 87,000 people, the island maintains a warm and tight-knit community culture where visitors are welcomed like family. The local language is Cebuano (Bisaya), and the people are known throughout the Philippines for their friendliness and hospitality.

Location

Camiguin is located in the Bohol Sea, approximately 80 kilometers north of Cagayan de Oro. It is part of the Northern Mindanao administrative region (Region X) of the Philippines.

Geography

The island covers 238 km² and is dominated by five volcanoes. Mt. Hibok-Hibok (1,332m) is the highest and most active. The island's volcanic origin gives it its exceptionally fertile soil and geothermal springs.

People and Culture

The Camiguinon people are warm, deeply Catholic, and proud of their heritage. Cebuano is the primary language. The island is known for its slow pace of life, safe environment, and genuine hospitality to visitors.
