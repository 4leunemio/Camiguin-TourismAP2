History of Camiguin

From pre-colonial settlements to Spanish colonization and volcanic upheaval — the story of Camiguin is written in fire, faith, and resilience.

A Rich and Turbulent Past

Camiguin's history stretches back thousands of years, with early Austronesian settlers establishing communities along its fertile volcanic shores. The island came under Spanish colonial rule in the 16th century, and the influence of that era is still visible today in its centuries-old churches.

Perhaps the most dramatic chapter in Camiguin's history occurred in 1871, when catastrophic volcanic eruptions from Mt. Vulcan reshaped the island's geography entirely, creating the iconic Sunken Cemetery that exists today.

🏛 Pre-Colonial Era

Indigenous Austronesian communities thrived on the island long before Spanish contact, living off the sea and the fertile volcanic soil of Camiguin's interior.

⛪ Spanish Colonial Period

Spanish missionaries arrived in the 1600s, establishing churches and converting the local population. Several of these colonial-era structures still stand as historical landmarks.

🌋 The 1871 Eruption

Mt. Vulcan's eruption caused massive destruction, sinking the old town and its cemetery into the sea — events that permanently changed the island's landscape and identity.
