About Camiguin

Learn about the Island Born of Fire — its geography, map, people, and what makes it one of the Philippines' most extraordinary destinations.

The Island Born of Fire

Camiguin is a small volcanic island province located in the Bohol Sea, off the northern coast of Mindanao in the Philippines. It is the second-smallest province in the country by land area, covering approximately 238 square kilometers, yet it boasts more volcanoes per square kilometer than any other island on Earth.

The province is divided into five municipalities: Mambajao (the provincial capital), Catarman, Guinsiliban, Mahinog, and Sagay. With a population of over 87,000 people, the island maintains a warm and tight-knit community culture where visitors are welcomed like family.

Location

Camiguin is located in the Bohol Sea, approximately 80 kilometers north of Cagayan de Oro. It is part of the Northern Mindanao administrative region (Region X) of the Philippines.

Geography

The island covers 238 km² and is dominated by five volcanoes. Mt. Hibok-Hibok (1,332m) is the highest and most active. The island's volcanic origin gives it its exceptionally fertile soil and geothermal springs.

People and Culture

The Camiguinon people are warm, deeply Catholic, and proud of their heritage. Cebuano is the primary language. The island is known for its slow pace of life, safe environment, and genuine hospitality.

Island Map & Navigation

Camiguin is one of the easiest islands in the Philippines to navigate, thanks to its single circumferential road that encircles the entire island. The road covers approximately 64 kilometers and passes through all five municipalities: Mambajao, Catarman, Guinsiliban, Mahinog, and Sagay.

The provincial capital, Mambajao, is located on the northern coast and serves as the main hub for accommodation, restaurants, government services, and transportation. The Benoni port is the main entry point for ferries arriving from Balingoan.

Key Landmarks

Major landmarks include White Island (off Yumbing, Mambajao), Ardent Hot Springs (Mambajao), the Sunken Cemetery (Catarman), the Old Gui-ob Ruins (Catarman), and Katibawasan Falls (Mambajao).

Ferry Port

The Benoni port in Mahinog is the main gateway to the island. From here, tricycles and habal-habal motorcycles are available to take you to your resort or destination.

Circumferential Road

The 64-kilometer ring road encircles the entire island and passes through all major towns and attractions. A full circuit by motorcycle takes approximately 2–3 hours depending on stops.

Practical Travel Tips

Camiguin is a relatively easy destination to travel to and around, but a few practical tips will help ensure your visit goes smoothly. The island has a relaxed pace and limited commercial infrastructure compared to more developed tourist destinations.

Communication

Mobile signal is available across most of the island, with Globe and Smart networks providing the most reliable coverage. Consider buying a local SIM card for better rates.

Money

ATMs are available in Mambajao, but it is advisable to bring enough cash as machines can run out during peak periods. Credit cards are rarely accepted outside major resorts.

Language

English is widely spoken in tourist areas. Cebuano and Tagalog are the local languages. Learning a few basic phrases is appreciated by locals.

Environment

Help preserve Camiguin's natural beauty by disposing of waste properly, avoiding damage to coral reefs, and supporting local businesses.
