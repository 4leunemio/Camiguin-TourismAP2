Destinations

Explore Camiguin's most iconic attractions — from pristine islands to volcanic peaks and natural hot springs.

White Island

A pristine uninhabited sandbar rising from turquoise waters, offering breathtaking views of Mt. Hibok-Hibok.

White Island is one of the most iconic natural attractions in the Philippines. This uninhabited white sandbar sits just offshore from Mambajao and is surrounded by crystal-clear waters of the Bohol Sea. Unlike most islands, White Island has no permanent structures—just powdery white sand, endless sky, and an unobstructed panoramic view of Mt. Hibok-Hibok.

The sandbar shifts and changes shape with the seasons and tides. At low tide, it stretches wide and inviting; at high tide, portions disappear beneath the sea. Visitors can wade in shallow waters, snorkel along the surrounding reef, or simply absorb one of the most serene landscapes the Philippines has to offer.

How to Get There

Take a short 10–15 minute boat ride from the White Island jump-off point near Yumbing, Mambajao. Bangka boats are available for hire at the beach.

Best Time to Visit

Early morning offers the calmest waters and fewest crowds. The dry season from March to June provides the best visibility and weather conditions.

Travel Tips

Bring sunscreen, water, and a hat—there is no shade on the sandbar. Snorkeling gear can be rented from boatmen. No food stalls are available on the island.

Mt. Hibok-Hibok

The highest peak on Camiguin at 1,332 meters, offering stunning views and an active volcanic experience.

Mt. Hibok-Hibok is the tallest volcano on Camiguin and one of the most accessible active volcanoes in the Philippines. The name "Hibok-Hibok" means "simmering" in the local Cebuano language, referring to the volcano's geothermal activity. The mountain continues to emit steam from fumaroles at its summit, a constant reminder of the geological forces that shaped Camiguin.

The hike to the summit takes approximately 4–6 hours round-trip and is popular among adventure seekers and nature lovers. The trail passes through different vegetation zones, from forest lowlands to alpine meadows, offering changing landscapes and panoramic views of the Bohol Sea, neighboring islands, and Mindanao.

Hiking Information

The trek is moderately challenging with steep sections. Guides are recommended, especially for first-time visitors. Start early to reach the summit for sunrise or before afternoon clouds roll in.

Volcanic Features

At the summit, you'll see active fumaroles (steam vents) and the crater rim. The geothermal heat, sulfur smell, and views across the Bohol Sea create an unforgettable volcanic experience.

What to Bring

Sturdy hiking boots, plenty of water, sun protection, a light jacket for the cooler summit, and a camera. The trail can be muddy, especially during rainy season.

Sunken Cemetery

A solemn and poignant memorial to the tragic 1871 volcanic eruption, where graves rest beneath the sea.

The Sunken Cemetery is one of Camiguin's most iconic and emotionally significant landmarks. Located in Catarman municipality, the cemetery sank beneath sea level during the catastrophic volcanic eruption of November 30, 1871, when Mt. Vulcan erupted and part of the island subsided. The event killed thousands and permanently altered the island's landscape.

Today, a white concrete cross rises above the water, marking the location of the graves beneath. The site serves as both a solemn memorial and a place of spiritual significance. Locals and visitors come to pray, reflect, and honor those lost in the tragedy. On All Saints Day (November 1st), the cemetery hosts a unique outdoor mass that is deeply moving for those who attend.

Respectful Visitation

The cemetery is a sacred site. Visitors are encouraged to approach with respect and reverence. Swimming or diving near the cross is generally discouraged out of respect for the graves.

Special Observances

All Saints Day (November 1st) and All Souls Day (November 2nd) bring special masses and commemorations. The Lanzones Festival (October) also includes events honoring the victims of the 1871 eruption.

Historical Significance

Visiting the Sunken Cemetery provides profound insight into Camiguin's volcanic history and the resilience of the Camiguinon people who rebuilt their lives after the disaster.

Ardent Hot Springs

Natural geothermal hot springs where volcanic heat warms the waters for a relaxing therapeutic experience.

Ardent Hot Springs is a unique natural attraction that showcases Camiguin's geothermal heritage. Located in Mambajao municipality, the springs feature naturally heated water fed by underground volcanic activity. The waters are rich in minerals and are believed to have therapeutic properties.

The spring area includes pools of varying temperatures where visitors can soak and relax. The cooler pools are refreshing, while the hotter pools provide a spa-like experience. Some facilities have been developed around the springs to provide comfort and convenience, though the area retains a natural, laid-back character typical of the island.

Thermal Experience

Multiple pools with different water temperatures allow visitors to choose their preferred warmth. The naturally heated mineral-rich waters are believed to provide health and relaxation benefits.

Mineral Properties

The geothermal waters contain minerals associated with traditional wellness practices. Many visitors soak specifically for the therapeutic and rejuvenating properties of the mineral-rich spring water.

Facilities & Access

Basic facilities are available, including changing rooms and picnic areas. The springs are easily accessible by road and are located within walking distance of Mambajao's main attractions.
