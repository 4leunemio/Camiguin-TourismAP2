Island Born of Fire

One of the Philippines' smallest provinces, Camiguin is a volcanic island paradise packed with natural wonders — from white sandbars and sunken cemeteries to hot springs and lush mountain trails.

White Island
Mt. Hibok-Hibok
Ardent Hot Springs

Rising from the sapphire waters of the Bohol Sea like a lush, emerald crown, Camiguin may be the second-smallest province in the Philippines, but its presence is nothing short of monumental. Known worldwide as the "Island Born of Fire," this unique landmass was forged through a series of dramatic volcanic eruptions that shaped its topography over millennia. Incredibly, this tiny pearl of an island holds more volcanoes per square kilometer than any other landmass on Earth. This fiery heritage has resulted in a landscape of startling contrasts, where the raw power of geothermal energy meets the serene beauty of a tropical paradise, creating a geological masterpiece that feels both ancient and alive.

The island's history is etched into its very geography, offering visitors a chance to witness the transformative power of nature firsthand. One can explore the hauntingly beautiful Sunken Cemetery, where a 19th-century graveyard was reclaimed by the sea following the birth of Mt. Vulcan, or trek the rugged slopes of Mt. Hibok-Hibok for a panoramic view of the archipelago. For those seeking relaxation, the island's volcanic heart provides a diverse menu of thermal wonders, from the steaming, sulfur-infused pools of Ardent Hot Springs to the bracing, crystal-clear depths of the Sto. Niño Cold Springs.

Beyond its physical wonders, Camiguin possesses a soulful charm rooted in the warmth of its people and the richness of its local traditions. Every year, the island bursts into color during the Lanzones Festival, celebrating the harvest of what many consider the sweetest fruit in the country. Life here moves at a gentler pace, where the noise of the modern world is replaced by the rhythmic pulse of the tides and the rustle of coconut palms.
