Island Born of Fire

One of the Philippines' smallest provinces, Camiguin is a volcanic island paradise packed with natural wonders — from white sandbars and sunken cemeteries to hot springs and lush mountain trails.

White Island
Mt. Hibok-Hibok
Ardent Hot Springs

Rising from the sapphire waters of the Bohol Sea like a lush, emerald crown, Camiguin may be the second-smallest province in the Philippines, but its presence is nothing short of monumental. Known worldwide as the "Island Born of Fire," this unique landmass was forged through a series of dramatic volcanic eruptions that shaped its topography over millennia.

The island's history is etched into its very geography, offering visitors a chance to witness the transformative power of nature firsthand. One can explore the hauntingly beautiful Sunken Cemetery, where a 19th-century graveyard was reclaimed by the sea following the birth of Mt. Vulcan, or trek the rugged slopes of Mt. Hibok-Hibok for a panoramic view of the archipelago.

Beyond its physical wonders, Camiguin possesses a soulful charm rooted in the warmth of its people and the richness of its local traditions. Every year, the island bursts into color during the Lanzones Festival, celebrating the harvest of what many consider the sweetest fruit in the country.
